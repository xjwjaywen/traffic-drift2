Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, “Collapse-Aware Maintenance of Encrypted Traffic Classifiers under Temporal Drift,” for publication in IEEE Internet of Things Journal.

Long-running traffic analytics can miss a failure in one class even when aggregate classifier performance remains acceptable. We characterize a class-asymmetric failure that we call absorber collapse and present CARE, a maintenance framework that combines class-health monitoring, limited target labeling, historical reference replay, and head-only repair. On CESNET-TLS-Year22, CARE raises macro-F1 from 0.629 to 0.700 and collapse-class F1 from 0.028 to 0.332 with 1,000 target labels under query-excluded evaluation. We also evaluate a QUIC protocol shift.

To examine the IoT scope directly, we include a separate, chronologically held-out device-type study across 11 residential networks. It shows recovery of low-recall device types and substantial variation across homes. We report both improvements and losses, compare a standard head-interpolation control, and identify the need to validate each candidate update before deployment. The IoT study evaluates the head-repair stage rather than a deployed gateway or the full monitoring workflow.

We believe the paper will interest readers studying reliable IoT traffic analytics, network manageability, and maintenance of learning systems under temporal drift. The manuscript and supplementary materials identify the original CESNET experiments and the separate IoT protocol and records.
